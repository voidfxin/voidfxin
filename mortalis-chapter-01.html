Chapter 01 — The Clock at 6:17

THE GREAT SILENCE

Eighty-three years ago, every clock in Elarion stopped at the exact same moment.

It was not an explosion. There was no warning in the sky, no army at the gates, no final message from the gods. One second, the world was moving. The next, every bell, every watch, every great tower-clock fell silent.

And then people discovered the second horror.

People no longer died.

The wounded healed, slowly or strangely. The old remained old, but could not pass beyond it. Grief lasted forever because the person you lost could still be standing beside you, unchanged by time, unable to explain why the world had become a cage.

They called that day The Great Silence. They built festivals around it, temples beneath it, and laws to pretend it was a blessing.

But in the abandoned district of Veyr, one clock still remembered how to move.

6:17
THE TIME THAT NEVER PASSED

Rain slid down the broken clocktower, tracing the cracks in its stone face. The hands pointed to 6:17. They had pointed there for eighty-three years.

A young man stood beneath the archway, his long black cloak shifting in the wind. Silver-black hair fell across his face. His crimson eyes watched the clock as if he had been waiting for it to speak.

His name was Rushi.

He looked young enough to belong to the new world. His eyes belonged to the old one.

“You are late,” he whispered.

The clock answered with a sound no living person in Elarion had heard for generations.

…TICK.

Dust shivered from the tower. Far above the clouds, the moon turned red. Across the city, thousands of clocks trembled in their housings, their hands twitching as if waking from a dream.

Rushi took one step back. For the first time in decades, something like fear crossed his face.

“No. Not yet.”

A voice came from the dark end of the street.

“You remember it too, don't you?”

A girl stood in the rain. Pale hair framed a face that seemed familiar in a way Rushi could not bear to examine. Her eyes were fixed on the clock, not on him.

“Who are you?” he asked.

“Eira.” She looked up at the red moon. “And when that clock strikes again, the thing that stole death will know you're still alive.”

Above them, the clouds opened. Something vast watched from behind the sky, an eye-shaped shadow stretching across the stars.

Rushi's hand tightened around the old silver token beneath his coat. He had spent eighty-three years trying to forget the world before the Silence.

Now the world was remembering him.

END OF CHAPTER 01
